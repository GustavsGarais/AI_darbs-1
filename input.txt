The Eiffel Tower: An Iconic Landmark
The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower located in Paris, France. It was designed by engineer Gustave Eiffel and constructed between 1887 and 1889 as the entrance arch for the 1889 World's Fair. The tower stands at a height of 324 meters (1,063 feet), making it the tallest structure in Paris.
Initially, the tower faced criticism from some of France's leading artists and intellectuals, who called it an eyesore. However, it has since become a global cultural icon of France and one of the most recognizable structures in the world. The Eiffel Tower is the most visited paid monument in the world, with approximately 7 million visitors annually.
The tower is divided into three levels accessible to visitors. The first and second levels feature restaurants, while the top level offers panoramic views of Paris up to 60 kilometers away on clear days. At night, the tower is illuminated with 20,000 light bulbs that create a spectacular golden glow visible throughout the city.
The structure weighs approximately 10,100 tons and is repainted every seven years to protect it from rust. During its construction, over 300 workers participated in assembling the 18,000 metallic parts held together by 2.5 million rivets. The Eiffel Tower was the world's tallest man-made structure for 41 years until the Chrysler Building was completed in New York in 1930.
Today, the Eiffel Tower serves multiple purposes beyond tourism. It hosts radio and television broadcasting antennas and has been used for scientific experiments, including early radio transmission tests and aerodynamic research. The tower has also become a symbol of romance, with thousands of marriage proposals taking place there each year.
